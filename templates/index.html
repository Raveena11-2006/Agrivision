Pipeline Architecture

How AgriVision Works

From raw photographic leaf capture to calibrated neural disease classification in 4 deterministic steps.

01

Upload Image

The user uploads a high-resolution or mobile photo of a single crop leaf under clear, natural lighting.

02

Image Preprocessing

The leaf image is cropped, converted to RGB, normalized into a 224×224×3 tensor array, and calibrated.

03

MobileNetV2 AI Model

Depthwise separable convolutions extract high-dimensional botanical pathology features with ultra-low latency.

04

Disease Prediction

A Softmax activation layer calculates calibrated probability scores across the 15 supported disease classes.

Project Background

Empowering Agriculture Through Computer Vision

AgriVision uses deep learning and computer vision to analyze crop leaf images and identify possible diseases. The system is designed to provide a quick and accessible first-level indication of crop health.

Early detection of foliar crop diseases is vital for preventing widespread yield losses and food insecurity. By deploying lightweight convolutional neural networks on standard consumer hardware, AgriVision bridges agricultural pathology expertise with accessible web technology.

15 Specific Classes Trained across key Solanaceae staples including Tomato, Potato, and Bell Pepper crops.
PlantVillage Benchmark Dataset Utilizes expert-annotated leaf imagery covering healthy tissues and diverse fungal, bacterial, and viral pathologies.
Transfer Learning with MobileNetV2 Leverages pre-trained feature extractors fine-tuned for minimal parameters and rapid edge inference.
Flask Micro-Service Backend Decoupled Python WSGI service handling image validation, tensor conversion, and API distribution.
Agricultural Advisory Notice: The system does not claim to provide a guaranteed diagnosis. It serves as an assistive preliminary diagnostic tool for field triage.

Model Transparency & Scope

Project Limitations & Ethical AI Notice

Essential operational guidelines for field usage, camera requirements, and model transparency.

Image Quality Dependency

Performance depends on image quality, illumination, camera focus, and clear visibility of the affected leaf area.

Trained Class Boundaries

The model is trained strictly on the 15 specific disease/healthy classes listed above and cannot identify other crops.

Out-of-Distribution Inputs

Non-leaf objects, background clutter, or non-target plant species will yield unreliable, out-of-distribution predictions.

Field vs Dataset Differences

Real-world field conditions with complex weather, shadows, or dual infections may differ from laboratory dataset conditions.

AI-Based Indication Only

Predictions should be treated as an AI-based indication, not a definitive agricultural or chemical treatment diagnosis.
